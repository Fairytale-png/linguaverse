Module 2
🔬 Characteristics of Human Language
Eight unique properties that set human language apart from all other communication systems.
01
🎲
Arbitrary
No natural connection between a word's sound and its meaning. "Dog" vs "anjing" vs "chien" yet all refer to the same animal!
02
♾️
Productive
From a finite set of words, you can create an infinite number of sentences, including ones never said before!
03
🔁
Duality of Patterning
Two levels: meaningless sounds (phonemes) combine to form meaningful units (morphemes/words).
04
🚀
Displacement
Talk about things far away in time and space: the past, the future, imaginary worlds.
05
🎭
Cultural Transmission
Language is learned through culture, not inherited biologically. Environment shapes the language we speak.
06
🎵
Vocal-Auditory Channel
The default channel for human language, though sign languages demonstrate channel flexibility.
07
🔄
Interchangeability
Speakers can both produce and receive language, unlike many animal communication systems.
08
🔄
Dynamic & Evolving
Language is not static; it changes over time with the society that uses it.
Module 3
🌿 Branches of Linguistics
Linguistics is not a single science; it has many branches, each focusing on a different dimension of language.
🔊
Phonology
Studies the sound system of language: how sounds function and distinguish meaning.
/p/ vs /b/ in "pin" vs "bin" = different phonemes!
🎙️
Phonetics
Studies language sounds physically: their production, acoustic properties, and perception.
Articulatory, acoustic, and auditory phonetics!
🧩
Morphology
Studies the internal structure of words and how morphemes form words.
"un-write-able" = 3 morphemes: un- + write + -able
🏗️
Syntax
Studies rules for combining words into grammatical phrases, clauses, and sentences.
"The cat eats fish" ✓ vs "Eats cat fish" ✗
💭
Semantics
Studies meaning in language: words, phrases, sentences, and meaning relationships.
"thin" vs "slim" share similar meaning yet carry different connotations!
💬
Pragmatics
Studies how context influences meaning: what is meant vs. what is literally said.
"Can you open the window?" = a request, not a question!
👥
Sociolinguistics
Studies the relationship between language and society: variation, diglossia, and social identity.
Javanese speech levels: ngoko, madya, krama!
🧠
Psycholinguistics
Studies language and human cognitive/psychological processes: acquisition and processing.
Aphasia: brain damage can selectively impair language!
🔬
Neurolinguistics
Studies how language is represented and processed in the human brain.
Broca's Area and Wernicke's Area are the brain's two primary language centers!
📜
Historical Linguistics
Studies language change over time and the reconstruction of proto-languages.
Indonesian, Malay, Tagalog → all from Proto-Austronesian!
